Project GoodPeople · A People plan for the next phase

Build the People system to power Goodera's next orbit jump

Goodera has grown on the speed, agility and ownership of its people, and much of how the company works still lives in conversations and in people's heads. This plan is about moving gradually towards systems while protecting the essence of the people who make Goodera what it is, so that the company keeps its pace as it grows.

Where we are

A people-heavy organisation that has run on the strength of its people, and is now ready to move steadily towards systems without losing what makes them special, while continuing to empower people.

What's missing

Objective measurement and shared accountability, which sit underneath the three gaps I see around resources, trust and merit.

What I'd build first

A cross-team 360, a performance system people can trust, and talent density that we plan for deliberately.

How it gets built

Together with Goodera's leaders and Gooders, starting with thirty days of listening, and with nothing launched until the people it affects are on board.

01 · Where we are

Why things are the way they are, and where we go from here

Goodera got to where it is because people moved fast, made decisions without waiting for permission and picked up whatever needed doing, and a good part of how the company runs today lives in the heads of the people who built it.

Some processes are stretched as a result, which is simply what building a company at this pace looks like, though the instincts that take a company from zero to one are rarely the ones that take it from one to a hundred. Across two such inflection points, I have learnt that the People system has to arrive just before the scale does, because too early it feels like bureaucracy and too late the company feels the gap when it can least afford to.

Ather Energy · 2018–2020~400 people

I built the company's first performance framework with the CTO and CHRO while Ather was getting ready to scale, and supported the CXO-led restructuring that came with that growth.

Emeritus · 2021–2026~1,000 people

I partnered teams across India, the US and APAC, moved performance onto OKRs, tied sales incentives to contribution margin and built a pipeline of leaders from within.

My vision for Goodera is to keep the speed, agility and ownership that got us here and make them repeatable, with goals, decisions and the talent bar written down and held to the same standard in Bengaluru and the US, guided throughout by Goodera's mission of making social impact accessible to every team on the planet. In a managed-delivery business our people and host network are the product, so I hold every proposal to one test, which is whether it makes a team faster or fairer.

How I look at what isn't working

The first question I ask of any problem is whether it is a people problem, a process problem or a tool problem, because each needs a very different answer. I have built the read below from the role brief, public signals and what I have seen at companies at a similar stage, and since I don't yet have internal data on attrition, eNPS or spans, the plan begins with a month of listening before anything is designed.

Three gaps that share one root cause

Each gap already carries a cost the business is paying today, and all three trace back to the same two missing pieces.

Resource gap

We can't yet tell a real shortage from an uneven load

Without a productivity baseline every request for headcount looks justified, so spans widen and our best managers end up stretched.

Cost and first move

What it costs us. Headcount grows faster than output, and the people carrying the heaviest loads tend to burn out first.

Where I'd start. A complexity-weighted baseline for each function, agreed before new hiring decisions.

Trust gap

We listen to our people but don't always close the loop

When people can't see what changed because of what they said, they gradually stop being candid and leadership loses touch with how the org feels.

Cost and first move

What it costs us. Problems and attrition signals surface late, usually after they have already cost us good people.

Where I'd start. Regular pulses and a townhall tracker that shows what we will do, what we can't do and why.

Meritocracy gap

We don't share one picture of what great looks like

Reward feels subjective, the last few months outweigh the rest of the year, and functions work in their own lanes without goals they share.

Cost and first move

What it costs us. Our strongest people can't see any difference in how they are treated, and they are usually the first to leave.

Where I'd start. One shared bar on speed, quality, complexity and culture, connected directly to reward and growth.

The leverage point

Two pieces that move all three gaps together

Objective measurement

A baseline and a small set of clear KPIs for each function, so that we can manage on evidence and use instinct where it adds to it.

Shared accountability

Outcomes owned together by the leadership team, so that every result has the whole team standing behind it.

Keeping the speed, and making it last

None of this asks teams to slow down, because the first moves go into fixing what is already slowing them, and every decision that follows is grounded in evidence that stays with the company as it grows.

02 · The plan

Listening first, then stabilising, then building a system that runs on its own

I have split the work into three horizons, each with a clear reason for being where it is, while culture, recognition and our employer brand run alongside all three.

First 30 days

Listen and learn

Designing before listening is how HR ends up slowing a company down, so the first month goes into understanding how people actually experience Goodera.

  • A listening tour across levels, functions, regions and tenures
  • One-on-ones, small focus groups split by tenure and region, and open office hours
  • A written read of the org that shapes every design choice that follows
0 to 90 days

Stabilise

These moves go after the gaps that are costing us the most today, while the business keeps its pace.

  • Run the Diagnostic 360 to see where teams are losing time with each other
  • An H2 leadership calibration with Dhruv that sets three to five outcomes per function, rolls them up to the org and puts the half's non-negotiables in writing
  • Map spans and agree a baseline for each function
  • Design the first performance cycle with each functional head
  • A regular pulse and a quarterly survey, with the loop closed in the open
3 to 18 months

Make it last

This is the work that lets growth carry on without depending on who is in the room.

  • A Next-in-Line programme for future team leads, and training for first-time managers
  • Succession mapped for every critical role
  • Processes and decision rights written down where everyone can find them
  • One system for goals, org data and talent
  • Great Place to Work certification by 2028

Alongside all three, with Marketing and TA

I think of culture as the organisation's operating system, and it is experienced far more in small everyday moments than in values written on a wall, which is why this work runs from the very start rather than waiting until the systems are in place.

Recognition tied to the bar

  • Instant peer recognition
  • Quarterly team and individual awards, named at the townhall
  • An annual achievers' circle grounded in performance data

Listening that closes the loop

  • A quarterly townhall with org, function and team spotlights
  • A monthly team rhythm owned by functional heads
  • An action tracker shared at every townhall

An employer brand people recognise

  • A LinkedIn employer brand built with Marketing
  • An internship pipeline built with TA
  • Humans of Goodera stories and properly run hackathons

What deliberately waits

A new HR tool, until the framework works on simple sheetsAny layers beyond team leadsThe GPTW application, until we have earned it

Keeping the speed, and making it last

The first ninety days go into fixing problems that are live today, and the eighteen months after that go into managers, succession and documentation, so that the company can keep moving on the strength of its systems as well as its people.

03 · Ground rules

The risks I am watching and the commitments I would ask us to hold

These are the guardrails I would keep in place while the plan runs, so that none of it costs Goodera the pace and the spirit that brought it here.

Growth brings change, and change is uncomfortable for most of us, so I would much rather we move through it with our people by being open about why it is happening than ask them to take it on trust.

Risks I'm watching

Leadership not aligned on why we are doing this

If the leadership team isn't aligned on the reason for change, everything downstream struggles, including the difficult calls that only leadership can make.

Two different standards across regions

A system that is lenient in one region and strict in another quietly erodes trust, so baselines can flex by team but never by geography.

Structure that ends up slowing teams

Anything that adds steps without making a team faster or fairer gets pulled back, however neat it looked on paper.

Knowledge that lives in people's heads

Over the longer term, as people grow into new roles or move on to new chapters, some of how Goodera works could go with them, and that is the dependency this plan sets out to reduce over time.

What we hold to

The mission stays where it is

Whatever we change has to serve making social impact accessible to every team, and nothing we introduce should pull us away from it.

Honesty with our people

We explain why change is needed and why each person matters to it, with HR carrying that message as a neutral and steady voice.

Nothing launches without buy-in

There will be pushback and some insecurity along the way, and I see working through it as part of my job, without diluting what we set out to do.

We can disagree and still commit

Leadership trusts each other and trusts HR, and every discussion ends with an agreed action and someone who owns it.

04 · The first system · Diagnostic 360

Understanding the friction between teams before we try to fix it

From what I can see, the way teams work across functions may be the most fragile part of the system today. The handoffs between Sales, Client Success, Experience and Logistics are where time and quality quietly get lost, and no individual performance review is designed to see them.

Every Gooder reviews the other teams they work with

They look at handoffs, responsiveness, shared ownership and how predictable the working relationship feels.

Each team receives a score along with the feedback behind it

This sits outside performance reviews, so that people can be candid about friction without it counting against a colleague.

Each functional head chooses two or three things to fix

I sit with them to understand the score, and every fix gets an owner and a date that we follow up on at the quarterly review.

05 · The second system · Performance and calibration

A performance system people can trust across the whole year

Why this section goes further than the others

I have gone into more detail on this system on purpose. The rating is where every future talent decision begins, from pay and promotion to succession and headcount. It is also where people decide whether they trust HR at all, so I wanted to show how it would work in practice rather than only describe it. The opening note and the four parts below give the full picture, and each part opens up if you would like the detail behind it.

I have sat in many rooms where performance is discussed in ratings and bell curves, and the conversations that stay with me are rarely about the numbers themselves.

For the person on the other side, a rating is tied to rent, EMIs, a parent's medical care and their own sense of dignity, which is why so many people walk into a review guarded and anxious, and why a system that feels opaque or political loses their trust very quickly. What I want for Goodera is a system objective enough that people believe in it and humane enough that the annual review feels like the natural end of a year-long conversation, rather than a verdict that arrives out of nowhere.

1 · How goals are setFewer KPIs that add up, goals that can be measured, and a conversation that runs through the year.
Fewer KPIs that add up

Mature teams usually have one primary KPI that matters most, with the rest feeding into it. Abhishek and his directs set three to five company outcomes, and every team's KPIs add up to its leader's with a sensible buffer built in.

Goals that can be measured

HR reviews every goal and pushes back gently on the vague ones, because "run better events" is a theme, whereas "keep the event tracker at least 98% complete, with no data errors at dispatch" is something a Program Manager can work towards.

A conversation through the year

Monthly self check-ins, a quarterly leadership review and a documented half-year conversation mean the rating reflects twelve months of work, and nobody hears something new for the first time in their review.

2 · How the work is scoredSpeed, quality and complexity read together, and Goodera's five values on the same four-point bar.

Speed

Whether the work landed by the deadline it actually needed.

+

Quality

How many iterations it took to get right, ideally just one.

+

Complexity

Low, medium or high, depending on how much precedent the task had.

Reading all three together protects the person taking on hard, unprecedented work from being outscored by someone who chose lighter work and presented it more loudly, and it lets us recognise judgement and collaboration alongside results without lowering the bar.

Below the bar

Falls short of what the role needs.

At the bar

Meets what the role needs reliably.

Above the bar

Consistently goes beyond it.

Sets the bar

Raises the standard and lifts others with them.

The same scale applies to the five values of Create impact at scale, Deliver an 11/10 experience, Make it happen, WE before ME, and Data + Imagination = Magic, with each level described in the same words across the whole org.

3 · How ratings are calibratedAn invisible bell curve that HR holds quietly, so ratings stay honest across functions.

HR holds this as a quiet sense-check, and nobody sees it as a forced ranking. Around a fifth of a team landing at the top is healthy, up to thirty percent is fine in the first cycle, and anything above forty or fifty percent tells us to look again at whether the bar was set too low. Above or setting the bar · top 20–25%

Growth

These are the people we look to first for promotions and stretch roles, matched to the benchmark of the level they move into.

Key roles and high impact

Reward

We pay above the median for key roles, using benchmarks refreshed every year from Radford, Korn Ferry, Mercer, Ravio and Figures.

Below the bar · 0–10%

Support, and a dignified exit if needed

A fair and structured improvement plan comes first, and where it still doesn't work out, the exit is time-bound and handled with respect.

06 · The third system · Talent density

Building talent density through design, with headcount following the evidence

A smaller group of strong people working to a clear bar will usually outperform a larger group that is stretched thin. When spans reach 1:20, managers spend their days queueing work rather than coaching people, and without a baseline every request for more people looks reasonable.

Understand the current state

Reporting lines, actual project loads and where roles overlap, in every region.

Map spans and layers

Every team above roughly 1:10 gets flagged for a closer look.

Bring co-dependent functions together

Where two functions depend on each other every day, we ask whether they would be better served by a single leader, using the 360 to see where that friction sits.

Design the team-lead layer

We define the missing roles and fill them from within wherever we can.

Clarify roles and decision rights

Who owns what is written down, so that accountability is clear to everyone.

Baseline each function and sequence the change

A complexity-weighted unit of work for each function, and a transition that is planned and communicated well ahead of time.

The case that makes it real

Two people with the same title and four times the difference in load

One program manager runs 120 projects while another runs 30, and the team asks for more headcount. Without a baseline the shortage looks real, whereas with one we can see whether we need more people or a better way of allocating the work we already have.

Manager and leadership capability

People tend to stay or leave because of their manager, so this means a Next-in-Line programme, first-time managers trained on feedback and the bar, succession mapped for every critical role, and co-dependent functions brought under one leader where that helps.

Workforce planning

Every hiring request goes through a build, grow or buy conversation, where we first ask whether someone here can be developed or moved into the role and only then look outside, and where AI can do or improve the work, we consider that first.

The bar, applied honestly

A fair improvement plan comes before any exit, top performers are given richer roles and responsibilities, and influential non-performers are handled with the same transparency as everyone else.

Hiring as a judgement call

Interviews use the same deliverables and culture scorecard that people are later reviewed on, with AI taking care of screening hygiene so that our interviewers can spend their time on judgement.

Built with Abhishek and the functional heads, using the 360 and the baselines as evidence, towards the right spans, a leaner leadership structure and headcount that follows real capacity.

Keeping the speed, and making it last

Fewer handoffs and clearer owners mean decisions move faster, and because leaders are developed from within and every critical role has a successor, the company keeps its momentum even as people move on.

07 · How I'll lead this

What this phase asks of its HR leader, and what I bring to it

Having set out what I would build, this is what I believe the phase asks of the person leading it. It needs someone who will sit with the leadership, the functional heads and even individuals while org design, headcount and role scope are being decided, and who can build the operating rhythm while the business keeps running at full speed. These are the four things I believe the role asks for, and you can open each one to see where I have done it before.

Starting from the business problemEvery People decision should answer a question about revenue, capacity, margin or risk before it answers an HR one.
Where I've done it · EmeritusI replaced a tenure-based sales incentive with one tied to contribution margin, which lifted productivity by 10–15%, added roughly $200–300K in revenue a quarter and brought incentive costs down by about $100K, and I ran the revenue productivity studies that shaped headcount and hiring budgets.
Building while the business runsA first version that works, improved cycle by cycle, serves a growing company far better than a perfect design that arrives a year too late.

Making hard calls with empathyI want to be the person a manager calls before a difficult conversation and the person an employee feels able to call after one.

Holding one bar across regionsA team spread across time zones should still feel like one team, with every Gooder held to the same standard and given the same chances to grow.

Built with the business

In the room as a partner

A rhythm that stays light

Monthly check-ins, quarterly leadership reviews and townhalls that end in agreed actions, with the exact cadence shaped once I am inside.

Keeping the speed, and making it last

Leaders continue to own their decisions while HR clears the path and brings the evidence, and because the system belongs to the leadership team as a whole, it never comes to depend on any one person, including me.
