Project GoodPeople · A People plan for the next phase

Build the People system to power Goodera's next orbit jump

A plan to move Goodera steadily towards systems while protecting the speed, agility and ownership of the people who built it.

Where we are

A people-heavy organisation where much of how work gets done lives in people's heads.

What's missing

Objective measurement and shared accountability, beneath three gaps in resources, trust and merit.

What I'd build first

A cross-team 360, a trusted performance system, clear levels and deliberate talent density.

How it gets built

With Goodera's leaders and Gooders, starting with 30 days of listening and launching nothing without buy-in.

01 · Where we are

Where Goodera is, and where it goes next

Goodera's People system needs to arrive just ahead of its next stage of scale.

Speed and ownership built the company, and some stretched processes are a natural result. Having been through two such inflection points, I have seen that a People system that arrives too early feels like bureaucracy, and one that arrives too late leaves a gap the company feels at the worst time.

Ather Energy · 2018–2020~400 people

Built the first performance framework with the CTO and CHRO as Ather prepared to scale.

Emeritus · 2021–2026~1,000 people

Partnered teams across India, the US and APAC, moved performance to OKRs and grew leaders from within.

My aim is to keep that speed and make it repeatable, with goals and the talent bar held to one standard across Bengaluru, the US and Barcelona. Every proposal is held to one test, whether it makes a team faster or fairer.

How I look at what isn't working

I first ask whether a problem is about people, process or tools. This read draws on the role brief, public signals and companies at a similar stage, so the plan starts with a month of listening.

Three gaps, one root cause

Resource gap

Goodera may be missing a clear view of capacity

Without a baseline, a real shortage and an uneven load can look exactly the same.

Cost and first move

Cost. Headcount grows faster than output, and the busiest people burn out first.

First move. A complexity-weighted baseline for each function.

Trust gap

Goodera may be missing open, honest feedback loops

Being transparent with teams and individuals about what is working and what isn't is what keeps people candid and trusting.

Cost and first move

Cost. Attrition signals surface late.

First move. Regular pulses, honest team-level readouts and a townhall action tracker.

Meritocracy gap

Goodera may be missing one shared picture of great

Without it, reward can feel subjective and functions drift into their own lanes.

Cost and first move

Cost. Top performers see no difference in how they are treated, and leave first.

First move. One bar on speed, quality, complexity and culture, tied to reward.

The leverage point

Two pieces move all three gaps

Objective measurement

A baseline and a few clear KPIs for each function.

Shared accountability

Outcomes owned together by Sriram and the leadership team.

03 · Ground rules

Risks to watch and commitments to hold

Change is uncomfortable, so we move through it openly with our people, without costing Goodera its pace or spirit.

Risks I'm watching

Leadership not aligned on why

Without alignment from Sriram and the leadership team, the hard calls stall.

Different standards by region

Baselines can flex by team, never by geography.

Structure that slows teams

Anything that doesn't make a team faster or fairer gets pulled back.

Knowledge that lives in people's heads

As people grow or move on, how Goodera works should stay with the company.

What we hold to

The mission stays central

Every change serves making social impact accessible to every team.

Honesty with our people

We explain why change is needed, with HR as a steady, neutral voice.

Nothing launches without buy-in

Disagree and commit

Every discussion ends with an agreed action and an owner.

05 · The second system · Performance and calibration

A performance system people can trust all year

Make the review the easy part.

By review time, everyone should already know where they stand.
That needs a system objective enough to believe in, where three to five KPIs per function roll up to company outcomes, work is scored on speed, quality and complexity, and ratings are calibrated across functions.
It also needs to be humane, with monthly check-ins and a documented half-year conversation, so the rating reflects twelve months of work and not just the last few weeks.
Done well, it decides who grows, keeps pay fair across regions and gives good people a reason to stay.

What a strong performance system unlocks

Growth

Promotions and stretch roles decided on the whole year of work.

Pay parity

The same level earns within the same band across regions, benchmarked every year.

Keeping the best

Top performers see a real difference in how they are recognised and rewarded.

Attracting talent

A fair, visible system is something strong candidates look for.

1 · How goals are setFewer KPIs that add up, measurable goals and check-ins through the year.
Fewer KPIs that add up

One primary KPI per function, with the rest feeding it. Abhishek and his directs set three to five company outcomes, and team KPIs roll up with a buffer.

Goals that can be measured

HR pushes back on vague goals. "Run better events" is a theme, while "keep the tracker 98% complete with no errors at dispatch" is a goal.

A conversation through the year

Monthly check-ins, a quarterly review with Sriram and the leadership team, and a half-year conversation, so the review holds no surprises.

2 · How the work is scoredSpeed, quality and complexity, plus the five values, on one four-point bar.

Speed

Landed by the deadline it needed.

+

Quality

Iterations to get it right, ideally one.

+

Complexity

Low, medium or high, by how much precedent existed.

Reading all three together protects people who take on hard work from being outscored by those who chose lighter work.

Below the bar

Falls short of the role.

At the bar

Meets the role reliably.

Above the bar

Consistently goes beyond.

Sets the bar

The same scale applies to Goodera's five values, described in the same words across the org.

07 · The fourth system · Talent density

Talent density by design, with headcount following evidence

Build density before headcount.
A small team of strong people, working to a clear bar, usually outperforms a large, stretched one.
At spans of 1:20, managers queue work instead of coaching.

Understand the current state

Reporting lines, project loads and overlaps in every region.

Map spans and layers

Teams above 1:10 get a closer look.

Bring co-dependent functions together

Where two functions depend on each other daily, consider one leader.

Design the team-lead layer

Define missing roles and fill them from within where possible.

Clarify decision rights

Who owns what, written down.

Baseline and sequence the change

A complexity-weighted unit of work per function, with change communicated early.

The case that makes it real

Same title, four times the load

One PM runs 120 projects, another runs 30, and the team asks for more headcount. A baseline shows whether Goodera needs more people or better allocation.
